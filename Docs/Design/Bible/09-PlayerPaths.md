# Bible 09 — Player Paths

The player's side of everything, as content. The progression *model* is
fixed (Framework §2.11: four ledgers — assets, standing, licenses,
knowledge; careers are descriptive labels; capability never permission,
DR-16/Pillar 3). This section defines the careers as content, the start,
and the ironman successor design (resolving OQ-6).

---

## 1. The Start (DR-16)

One battered **Ghtroc 720** (Bible 05), a few hundred credits, a Rim-world
dock, and a galaxy three days into a war. No tutorial, no quest marker —
the onboarding *is* the small-contract surface (Bible 07 §4): a fixer who
knows a trader short a ship, a market board, a HoloNet feed telling you —
late — where the war went yesterday. Start world: proposed in
`AuthoredSystemsShortlist.md` (player-start candidates flagged; final call
at Phase 2 content).

## 2. The Careers

Each career: entry, loop, milestone capabilities (what accumulating
ledgers unlock in practice), and what it rides. Careers arrive with their
phases — the galaxy runs all of them abstractly from Phase 3–4 regardless;
the *player's door* opens when the phase ships the verbs.

### 2.1 Trader (Phase 3)
- **Entry:** the starter hold and a price board.
- **Loop:** buy low → fly real lanes through a real war → sell where the
  spread is (news-lagged prices mean *knowledge is margin*).
- **Milestones:** bigger holds → multiple ships (hired agent crews) →
  standing contracts → the YT-1300.
- **Rides:** Framework §2.5–2.6 wholesale. The NPC traders are the
  player's mirror and competition by construction.

### 2.2 Smuggler (Phases 3–4)
- **Entry:** the first contraband tag in the hold; the first inspection
  regime on a lane (Framework §2.1).
- **Loop:** margin vs. signature — route around inspections (Local edges),
  bribe, or run. Black Sun and Hutt fences (Bible 03) are the demand side.
- **Milestones:** fence networks → low-signature hulls → a *reputation for
  discretion* (standing with underworld factions is the license that
  matters).
- **Rides:** contraband law (§2.4), routing cost functions, Bible 06's
  black-market layer.

### 2.3 Bounty Hunter (Phase 5)
- **Entry:** BHG accreditation (a license, revocable — Bible 03 §4).
- **Loop:** board → intel (sightings are tradeable knowledge, Bible 07
  §4) → pursuit → take them warm or cold.
- **Milestones:** guild rank tiers → high-bond targets → hunting *Persons*
  (with everything Bible 07 §3 implies when one dies).
- **Rides:** BHG faction, knowledge ledger, combat (Phase 5), the war's
  criminal runoff (P6 disorder posts the bounties).

### 2.4 Escort & Mercenary (Phase 5)
- **Entry:** convoy postings (Bible 08 §7) — the war's honest work.
- **Loop:** contract → protect the tonnage → get paid by survival.
- **Milestones:** retainer contracts → faction auxiliary work (a
  *marque* short of enlistment) → your own escort company (agents flying
  your wing).
- **Rides:** convoy demand (§2.6), letters of marque, encounter
  generation from sim state (Roadmap Phase 5's whole point).

### 2.5 Pirate (Phase 5)
- **Entry:** any armed hull and worse judgment.
- **Loop:** hunt where traders route and patrols don't (the same math the
  pirate agents read — the player *is* an agent to the sim).
- **Milestones:** fences → a crew → Corsair Compact standing → *being the
  reason a lane's insurance rate moved*.
- **Cost:** bounties on you are the same system as §2.3, pointed the other
  way. Piracy is a career whose exit narrows — deliberately.

### 2.6 Soldier → Officer → Commander (Phase 5 → 6+, DR-11)
- **Entry:** enlist with either major (or a minor's militia); rank is a
  faction grant (license ledger).
- **Loop (enlisted):** deployments as encounters inside abstract
  operations — you fight where the sim says the front is.
- **Loop (officer/commander):** the same fleet-order verbs the faction AI
  uses (Framework §1.6), issued by you: patrol, escort, blockade, invade
  — with the operations budget you're trusted with. The abstract invasion
  sequence (Bible 08 §5) becomes *your* checklist.
- **Milestones:** rank grants → task-force command → sector command;
  pilotable capitals arrive here naturally (DR-13: your flagship's helm
  is yours).
- **Rides:** everything in Bible 08. This is the Mount & Blade path.

### 2.7 Industrialist (late Phase 5+, DR-15)
- **Entry:** first owned production slot or station share.
- **Loop:** own the supply the war and the trade lanes demand; hire the
  agents (haulers, escorts) other careers *are*.
- **Milestones:** production slots → stations → shipyard shares → being a
  *market mover* whose decisions the economy prices.
- **Rides:** Framework §2.5's ownership-is-an-ID design; no new systems.

### 2.8 Politician (Phase 6+)
- **Entry:** citizenship, standing, and money where a government's
  policy verbs live (Framework §2.4).
- **Loop:** policy as gameplay — patrol budgets, tariffs, neutrality
  votes; the Senate during a centralization ratchet (Bible 02 P4) is a
  gameboard all by itself.
- **Milestones:** local office → sector influence → the rooms where the
  war is steered — or exposed (evidence chains end in hearings as often
  as in duels).

### 2.9 Explorer & Information Broker (cross-phase, quiet default)
- **Entry:** a hyperdrive and the 4,100 catalog systems nobody visits.
- **Loop:** survey data, route knowledge, sightings, rumor freshness —
  the knowledge ledger monetized (Bible 07 §4's buyers exist already).
- **Why it matters:** it's the career that needs *no combat and no
  capital* — the proof that presence-not-protagonism (Vision) has a
  playstyle.

### 2.10 The Force Path (Phase 6+, DR-08)
- Latent trait, probed by Phase 6+ content; the Jedi (or darker) door
  opens *on top of* everything above — reputation, factions, combat,
  the war's moral ledger — which is why it ships last (Roadmap). Not
  specced further here by design.

## 3. Ironman Succession (resolves OQ-6)

Opt-in mode (DR-17): death continues the *same galaxy* as a successor.

- **The successor** is a named beneficiary — chosen at creation (a cousin,
  a partner, a first mate) and re-designatable at any dock (a will is a
  one-line menu item, and quietly one of the mode's best storytelling
  knobs).
- **Assets:** registered property (docked ships, stations, balances)
  transfers minus an **estate friction** cut (fees, probate, theft — the
  Rim's inheritance tax). Whatever was *on* you — the ship you died in,
  the cargo in its hold — is lost or salvage (Bible 08 §7; yes, your own
  wreck enters the salvage economy).
- **Standing:** factions remember the *estate*, not the soul: standing
  decays sharply toward neutral (institutions care less about your heir
  than they cared about you); personal Person relationships close;
  grudges against your *organization* (bounties on your company, Corsair
  feuds) persist.
- **Licenses:** void. Ranks, accreditations, marques are re-earned — the
  successor is a different citizen.
- **Knowledge:** the ship's charts survive where the ship does; the
  *social* knowledge (rumor networks, fence trust) dies with you. Maps
  are property; relationships aren't.
- **Where you resume:** wherever the beneficiary plausibly was — their
  home dock, your registered estate — never where you died.
- **The galaxy:** does not blink. The war does not pause for your
  funeral. That's the product.

## Pillar Check

- **3 — Freedom:** ten doors, no classes, every ledger reversible;
  the quiet Explorer default proves the no-capital path exists. *Serves.*
- **1 — Lives:** every career's counterparty is an agent that exists
  anyway; the player is one more ID in systems that never check for
  protagonists. *Serves.*
- **5 — Immersion:** succession keeps the one galaxy continuous through
  the player's own death — the pillar's strongest possible statement.
  *Serves.*

## Scalability Check

- Ten careers, zero career-specific systems: every loop is existing verbs
  + existing agents + phase-gated UI. New careers cost content, not
  architecture. *Passes.*
- Succession costs one save-schema field cluster and an estate-transfer
  routine. *Passes.*
